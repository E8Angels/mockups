# Sector & Tags

## Background

Companies carry a primary category (`companies.category`, one value) and secondary categories (`companies.secondary_category`, a JSON array). Both are generated by `lib/ai-categorizer.js` from tagline, problem, solution and business model on the first application submit. They have two problems:

- The primary list mixes sectors (Water, Transportation) with business types (Software, Finance), so the same kind of company lands in different places. Sector stayed the same on all three runs for 89% of applications under the new rules, against a list that has the two mixed together today.
- The secondary list is flat, so a company tagged "Anodes" is invisible to a search for "Batteries".

The redesign was tested on the 1,080 applications added since 2024-09-19. Taxonomy review page: https://claude.ai/artifact/TSe7kFJEPMh1Jh3DCKGC86. Jordan may gather team feedback on the lists in a Google Sheet. If he shares it, fold it into the WP0 definitions. The lists stay editable after launch, so implementation does not wait on that feedback.

## Decisions

1. **Sector** replaces "Category" / "Primary category" in every user-facing place: UI, exports, Ask AI, MCP and docs. It is also renamed in the schema (`companies.sector`) so future agents don't meet two names for one thing. It holds exactly one value and adds up to 100% in reporting.
2. **Tags** replace "Secondary categories". They come in four families:
   - **Technology:** a tree up to 3 levels deep. Only the most specific tag is stored; every level above it is implied.
   - **Market:** flat, as many as genuinely apply: who the company sells to or intends to work with. Broad values like Government are useful for combining with other filters.
   - **Business type:** flat, exactly 1. Defined as *what customers pay for*.
   - **Built with:** flat. The underlying capability the product relies on, in any domain: AI, sensors, robotics, biotech and so on. It includes *Biotech & synthetic biology*, moved out of Business type. Technology answers "what are they building?" and Built with answers "what is it built with?". For example, *Technology: Desalination*, *Built with: AI / machine learning*. Each definition says which family a borderline concept belongs to: *Biomanufacturing* is Technology, *Biotech* is Built with.
3. Sector list changes:
   - Rename "Industry" to **Industrial**.
   - Drop Software, Finance and Energy Efficiency.
   - Add Industrial, Clean Fuels & Hydrogen and Not cleantech.
   - Rename Recycling to Recycling & Waste, and Infrastructure to Grid & Power.
   - The full list and its rules are in the appendix.
4. Each **application** is classified on its own, using its AI summary when one exists and its form fields otherwise.
   - The **company** shows the Sector of its latest application and the union of all its applications' tags (see Re-applications).
   - Dealflow reports count each application under its own Sector.
5. **AI results are saved, not recomputed.**
   - A person's Sector choice is never overwritten by AI.
   - A tag a person adds stays; a tag a person removes never comes back.
   - New applications only add tags (see Re-applications).
6. Meaning-based search (vectors) is a later, separate plan. It runs on the full application text, with tags as filters. This plan only has to leave the data ready for it.

## Filtering and search

- **One shared tag picker** (`src/components/ui/tag-picker.jsx`), built on Popover + cmdk following the `region-combobox.jsx` pattern:
  - **With no search text:** a browsable tree, grouped by family, with expand/collapse arrows and checkboxes.
  - **With search text:** matches any level of the tree, plus each tag's synonyms. Each result shows its path. Name-prefix matches come first, then shallower tags.
  - **Selecting a parent** selects its whole branch. Its children show as checked and disabled ("included").
  - Selected tags show as removable chips at the top.
  - **Keyboard:** arrows, Enter and Escape.
  - **Scope:** it can be limited to one family (admin grid fields) or cover all four (Explore).
- **Matching:** each company row carries `tag_ids_expanded`, which is its stored tags plus every ancestor. A filter on any tag is then an ordinary `has_any_of` / `has_all_of` / `has_none_of` against that array. The existing grid filter code (`_applyAdminGridFiltersToRows`, `evaluateRowColorRule`) needs no knowledge of the tree.
- **Admin grid fields**, on companies, applications and portfolio:
  - `sector` (single select)
  - `technology_tags`, `market_tags`, `business_type` and `built_with_tags`, each a multi select that uses the tag picker as its value control
  - Options come from the taxonomy, not from the values present in the rows.
- **Explore Companies:** a Sector dropdown (existing `MultiSelectDropdown`) and a Tags dropdown (the tag picker, all families). The URL takes `?sector=` and `?tag=` for links from pills and Metrics. Free-text search matches tag names, including ancestor names.
- **Stage Review:** its "Primary Category" filter becomes Sector.
- **Pitch History:** text search matches Sector and tag names.
- **Metrics:** "By Category" becomes "By Sector". The XLSX header becomes "Sector" and the sheet "By Sector".

## Data model

Use expand/contract so old and new code can run side by side. Production runs on more than one machine, so a rename in place would break whichever machines are still on old code during the deploy.

- **`companies`:**
  - Add `sector TEXT` and `sector_source TEXT` (`ai` | `manual`).
  - Copy `category` into `sector` for existing rows. Old values that are no longer valid stay until the backfill replaces them.
  - Drop `category` and `secondary_category` in the cleanup phase.
- **`taxonomy_tags`:**
  - Columns: `id` (stable slug, never reused), `family` (`technology` | `market` | `business_type` | `built_with`), `name`, `parent_id`, `description`, `includes`, `excludes_json` (see-also tag ids with a note), `synonyms_json`, `sort_order`, `active`, and timestamps.
  - The definition fields are also the prompt text. Seeded from `lib/taxonomy/seed.json` (format in `orchestrator-brief.md`).
  - A `taxonomy_versions` row (version number, changed_at, changed_by, summary) is added on every edit.
  - A rename changes `name` only.
  - A tag that is retired is deactivated, never deleted. It can optionally be merged into another tag, which moves its company links.
- **`company_tags`:** (`company_record_id`, `tag_id`) as the primary key, plus `is_primary` (exactly one Technology tag per company), `votes` (1–3), `source` (`ai` | `manual`), `from_application_record_id`, `created_at` and `created_by_person_record_id`.
- **`application_classifications`:** one row per application, the AI result for that application. Columns:
  - `sector`
  - `sector_agreement` (3/3 or 2/3)
  - `tags_json` (tag ids with votes)
  - `taxonomy_version`, `prompt_version`, `model`
  - `needs_review`, `rationale`, `suggested_tag`, `input` (`summary` | `form`) and `classified_at`

  The company's Sector and Tags are rolled up from these rows (see Re-applications).
- **`company_tag_exclusions`:** (`company_record_id`, `tag_id`, `removed_by`, `removed_at`). A tag a person removed never comes back from AI.
- **`application_classification_briefs`:** per application, the brief JSON, model and created date. Vector search will reuse it later.
- **Managed lists:** the list key `category` becomes `sector`, labelled "Sector", and is re-seeded with the new 16 values and definitions. The `subcategory` list is retired in cleanup. Sector keeps its managed colours.
- **Cache / directory / grid rows:** add
  - `sector`
  - `tags`: `[{id, family, name, path}]`, leaves only
  - `tag_ids_expanded`: leaves plus ancestors
  - `tag_search_text`: all names on each path, for text search
- **API:**
  - `GET /api/taxonomy`: the tree and flat families, with synonyms and active flag. Cached, and refreshed through the cache-invalidation log.
  - `PATCH /api/sourcing/company/:id`: accepts `sector` and `tags` (the full desired array of tag ids).
    - A Sector change sets `sector_source = manual`.
    - An added tag is written with `source = manual`.
    - A removed tag is deleted and recorded in `company_tag_exclusions`.
  - Existing company and application payloads return `sector` and `tags` in the row shape above.
- **Saved views:** a migration rewrites stored grid field keys (`category` → `sector`) in saved views and query plans. Filters on `secondary_category` can't be mapped automatically. They are dropped, and the migration prints each affected view so it can be rebuilt.
- **Merge:** `sector` and `company_tags` are added to the company merge lists. The coverage test in `__tests__/lib/company-merge-coverage.test.js` enforces this.

## AI tagging

Sector and Tags are two separate classifications. Sector is not derived from Technology.

What the test run showed:

- **Sector** held on 3 of 3 runs for 89% of applications. Nearly all the variation fell on three boundaries.
- **Tag stability** was never measured: tags were generated only once.
- **"Hardware + software"** landed on 39% of applications and told us nothing, so it is dropped.

There will be no human-labelled test set. Accuracy therefore rests on three things:

- written definitions
- agreement between strong reference models from two vendors
- run-to-run agreement

### Taxonomy rules

- **Every Sector and every tag gets a written definition.** Each one has:
  - *includes*
  - *excludes → see X*
  - *synonyms*

  The definitions are the contract between today's model and any future one: a new model is judged on whether it applies the same definitions, not on whether it matches old outputs. They are also where the three unstable Sector boundaries get settled:
  - Industrial vs Sustainable Materials
  - Energy Storage vs Grid & Power
  - Recycling & Waste vs Sustainable Materials
- **Market:** as many values as genuinely apply. It means who the company sells to or intends to work with. Tighter definitions, but no cap.
  - "Government & municipalities" means government as a buyer or deployment partner, not only as a grant source.
  - "Heavy industry & manufacturing" means industrial customers.
  - "Builders & developers" splits in two: **Real estate & construction** (buildings) and **Energy project developers** (companies that build solar, wind, storage and similar projects, as customers).
  - Business type keeps its separate value, *Project developer / operator*, for a company that develops projects itself.
- **Business type** follows what customers pay for. "Hardware + software" is dropped: a device with an app is Hardware, and a subscription is Software.
- **Built with:** a capability is tagged when it is the product or fundamental to the pitch. For AI, that means AI is what's being sold, or the pitch depends on it; an incidental mention doesn't count.

### Pipeline

`lib/company-tagging.js` replaces `ai-categorizer.js`. Models are chosen through `lib/ai-models.js`, which gets a new `tagging` role.

1. **Brief.** One call turns the application into a short structured description: what they do, the technology path in their own words, applications, customers, and what they sell. It is stored per application as `application_classification_briefs`.
   - The test run showed that mapping this normalized text to the taxonomy is more consistent than mapping raw applications of very different lengths (summary vs form fields).
   - Vector search will embed the same brief later.
2. **Classify.** Structured output with enums over the active Sector list and taxonomy, including every definition. The static definitions come first so the prompt is cached. It returns:
   - Sector
   - Technology tags (most specific that fits)
   - Market, Business type and Built with
   - a one-line rationale
   - an optional `suggested_tag` when nothing fits
3. **Vote.** Step 2 runs 3 times.
   - **Sector:** majority. A 2–1 split marks the company `needs_review`.
   - **Tags:** the default is the **union**, any tag returned by any run. Missing a company in a search costs more than an occasional extra match.
   - Each tag stores how many runs returned it (`votes` 1–3). Similar-company ranking weights 3-vote tags above 1-vote tags, and the health report watches the 1-vote share.
   - WP0 tests union against 2-of-3 on the reference set, measuring recall, precision, tags per company, and run-to-run agreement of the final set. Union ships unless it clearly over-tags.

**Every assignment records** `taxonomy_version`, `prompt_version`, `model`, `agreement` and the source application. When a node is split later, only the companies on that node are re-tagged.

**When classification runs.** Once per application, when the best input is ready.

- **With a pitch deck:** the portal already generates the AI summary in the background after submit (`startPitchDeckPipelineInBackground`). Classification runs at the end of that pipeline.
- **Without a pitch deck:** classification runs at submit, from the form fields.
- **One exception:** a company classified from form fields only is classified again once, if a summary later appears (the scheduled backfill or screening review can generate one). That second run only happens because the input got much better, and it costs a fraction of a cent.

Later summary regenerations do not trigger re-classification. A trigger never overwrites a `manual` field.

### Re-applications

When a company that is already tagged submits a new application, the new application is classified on its own and stored in `application_classifications`. Then the company roll-up is recomputed:

- **Tags are additive:** the union of every application's tags.
  - A tag is never dropped because a newer application doesn't mention it.
  - Removing tags is manual only. A removal is recorded in `company_tag_exclusions` so AI never re-adds it.
  - Each tag keeps its highest vote count across applications.
- **Sector:** the company shows the Sector of its **latest** application, unless a person set it manually. A pivot is a real change.
  - When a re-application's Sector differs from the previous one, it appears in the monthly report under "Sector changes", so pivots are visible rather than silent.
- **Reporting:** dealflow counts use **each application's own Sector**. A 2024 application stays in the Sector it was classified as in 2024, even if the company later pivots, so historical reports don't shift.

### Model choice

Prices are per million tokens, input / output, at standard rates as of 2026-09-25. Batch processing halves them.

| Tier | OpenAI | Anthropic |
|---|---|---|
| Top | GPT-6 Astra (about $10 / $50) | Claude Fable 5.1 ($10 / $50) |
| Flagship | **GPT-6 Sol** ($2 / $10) | **Claude Opus 5.5** ($4 / $20) |
| Mid | GPT-5.6 Terra (about $2 / $12) | **Claude Sonnet 5** ($2 / $10) |
| Small / high-volume | **GPT-6 Luna** ($0.10 / $0.50), GPT-5.6 Luna (about $0.20 / $1.20) | **Claude Haiku 4.5** ($1 / $5, previous generation) |

- **Reference models:** one flagship per vendor, GPT-6 Sol and Claude Opus 5.5, each at high effort. The top tier isn't needed for a labelling job this size.
- **Production candidates:** GPT-6 Luna, GPT-5.6 Luna, Claude Haiku 4.5 and Claude Sonnet 5.
  - Anthropic has no current-generation small model. Haiku 4.5 is a generation older and may be too small, so Sonnet 5 is tested alongside it.
  - The WP0 test decides. Nothing is assumed from price.
- The portal already has keys for both vendors (`OPENAI_*`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`). Its current classification model, `gpt-5-nano`, is from an older generation.

**What each vendor is used for:**

- **Production runs on one model from one vendor**, with the 3-run vote. Using both vendors on every application would double the integration work and still need a tie rule, without making the result more repeatable.
- **Both vendors are used where their disagreement is informative:**
  - building the reference set
  - the monthly audit
  - model-change checks

  When two vendors read the same definition differently, the definition is ambiguous. That is the most useful signal the system can produce without human labels.

### Accuracy without human labels (WP0)

1. **Reference set.** About 200 applications from the two-year sample:
   - Stratified across sectors, the known boundary cases, and form-only as well as summary-based applications.
   - Each is classified at high effort by one flagship model from each vendor (GPT-6 Sol and Claude Opus 5.5).
   - Where the two agree, that is the reference label.
   - For Sector, where they disagree, the WP0 agent reviews the case against the definitions and records its reasoning in the results note. Each disagreement either tightens a definition or is recorded as a genuine boundary case.
   - For tags, the reference keeps both the union and the intersection of the two models' tags, so recall and precision can both be measured.
   - This costs a few dollars.
2. **Candidate test.** Each small candidate classifies the whole set 3 times. Measured:
   - agreement with the reference (Sector, then Technology at the second level and exact, Market overlap, Business type)
   - run-to-run agreement
   - cost per application
3. **Pick the cheapest model that clears the bar:**
   - Sector: at least 90% agreement with the reference, and at least 95% run-to-run.
   - Technology at the second level: at least 85% agreement with the reference.

   If none clears it, fix definitions before switching to a bigger model.
4. **Frozen.** The reference set is frozen and versioned, and becomes the regression check for any future change to the taxonomy, prompt or model.

### Keeping it healthy over time

On the 1st of each month, a job in the worker builds a **Taxonomy health report**:

- **Where it lives:** saved as a monthly snapshot and viewable on a portal page, **Admin → Lists → Taxonomy health**. The page keeps past months.
- **Notification:** a Slack message with the link to `#screening-chairs-sarah-jordan`, channel ID `C0B8LQD6SN4`, via the existing `lib/slack-notifier.js` and a new env var `SLACK_TAXONOMY_REPORT_CHANNEL_ID`. The E8 bot is confirmed as a member, checked with the dev environment's bot token. Production must use the same bot.

**What the report covers:**

- **Trends:** counts per Sector and per tag, this month vs the trailing 12 months, with rising and fading themes flagged.
- **Weak spots:**
  - 2–1 Sector votes and `needs_review`, broken down by Sector
  - the share of 1-vote tags, by tag
  - the share of "Other"
  - tags over about 40 companies (split candidates) and under 3 (merge candidates)
- **Gaps:** the month's `suggested_tag` values, clustered by a model into proposed new tags with example companies.
- **Cross-vendor audit:** 30 of the month's new applications are classified again by the other vendor. The disagreement rate is broken down by definition.
- **Model drift:** the reference set is re-run on the production model, catching silent provider changes and upcoming deprecations.

**Review:** Jordan reviews each month's report.

- The report ends with a short list of **proposed changes**: add, split, merge, or edit a definition. Each comes with its evidence.
- Each change is accepted or dismissed on the page. An accepted change is applied through the Lists admin, the taxonomy version goes up, and only the affected companies are re-tagged. The reference labels for the changed nodes are regenerated.
- Most months will have nothing to accept.

**Changing models** (a deprecation or a better model): the new model runs the reference set, and it's accepted only if it clears the same bar.

### Backfill

- **Script:** `scripts/backfill-sector-and-tags.js` classifies every company from its latest submitted application.
  - Uses the batch APIs.
  - Resumable, with `--dry-run` (a report of old vs new, with counts) and `--limit`.
  - Never overwrites `manual`.
- **Cost for ~3,150 applications** (brief + 3 votes):
  - under $10 on GPT-6 Luna or GPT-5.6 Luna
  - roughly $40–60 on Claude Haiku 4.5

  Afterwards each new application costs well under a cent.
- **Missing briefs:** applications with no AI summary use their form fields to build the brief.

### AI Insights report

The only place the report depends on categories is its closing "Similar E8 applications" section.

**How it works today:**

- `getSimilarCategoryApplications` (`lib/cache-manager/application-insights.js`) returns every other company with the *same primary category*. Portfolio companies come first, then the rest by recency, capped at 20 per group.
- The bundle includes `primary_category`.
- The prompt (`docs/application-insights-prompt.md` §"Similar E8 applications", plus the stored `screening_review` prompt) tells the model the list is "the same primary category", to keep the order, and to "name the shared primary category in the section caption".

With 100+ companies in a sector, "the 20 most recent" is close to arbitrary.

**Changes:**

- **Evidence** (renamed `getSimilarApplications`):
  - Candidates are companies with the same sector *or* at least one shared Technology branch. This catches cross-sector look-alikes, such as a battery-materials company that landed in Industrial.
  - Score: same leaf tag 3, same level-2 branch 1.5, shared Market 0.5, same Sector 0.5. Keep only scores ≥ 3.
  - Keep the portfolio/other split and the 20-per-group cap. Order within each group by score, then recency.
  - Each item gains `sector` and `shared_tags` (the most specific shared tag names).
  - The bundle replaces `primary_category` with `sector` and `match_basis: "tags"`.
- **Prompt** (version bump, stored with `scripts/migrate-application-insights-prompt.js`; the prod prompt write needs Jordan's approval):
  - Describe the list generically: "other companies E8 has seen that are most similar to this one, already ordered". Don't name the method, so the later vector-search phase can switch `match_basis` to `"meaning"` without another prompt change.
  - Add a third column, **Why similar**, rendered from `shared_tags` (or `match_reason` once vectors land).
  - Replace "name the shared primary category in the section caption" with "name this company's sector in the section caption".
  - Keep the order, the truncation captions and the "don't cite this section" rule unchanged.
- **Tests:** update the similar-applications cases in `__tests__/lib/application-insights.test.js` for scoring, cross-sector matches, the per-group cap and ordering.
- **Existing reports** are not regenerated. Their similar sections stay as generated.
- No other part of the prompt uses category. The model already receives the full company row, so it gets `sector` and `tags` automatically.

## Appendix: Sector list and rules

The starting Tags list is the `TECH` and `FLAT` constants in `mockup.html` (keys `business` → `business_type`, `enabling` → `built_with`). WP0 turns it into `lib/taxonomy/seed.json`.

| Sector | Definition |
|---|---|
| AgTech & Food | Farming, livestock, aquaculture, ag inputs, crop and soil tech, food production and processing, alternative proteins, food waste reduction, food cold chain. |
| Built Environment | Buildings and construction: HVAC and heat pumps, envelope and controls, construction methods and building products, housing, data center cooling, building electrification. |
| Carbon | Carbon removal and capture, carbon credits and markets, carbon/emissions accounting and reporting, methane detection and abatement. |
| Clean Fuels & Hydrogen | Hydrogen production, storage and fuel cells; ammonia; biofuels, SAF, RNG and biogas, synthetic fuels. Fuels land here even when used in transport. |
| Conservation & Adaptation | Biodiversity, forestry, oceans and reefs, land restoration, climate risk and resilience, wildfire, extreme heat, disaster response, weather and climate-risk analytics. |
| Energy Generation | Electricity or heat from solar, wind, geothermal, hydro, nuclear, marine and other sources, including components, installers, community solar and project finance for generation. |
| Energy Storage | Batteries, battery materials and components, battery management, thermal, mechanical and other storage, storage projects, regardless of customer. |
| Environmental Cleanup | Remediation of soil and air, air quality, toxics, pollution monitoring that is not water- or carbon-specific. |
| Grid & Power | Transmission and distribution, grid software and planning, VPPs, DER management, demand response, microgrids, power electronics, backup and off-grid power, utility operations, electricity retail. |
| Industrial | Decarbonizing industrial production: cement, steel, chemicals, industrial heat and steam, process efficiency and equipment, mining, critical minerals, manufacturing processes. |
| Recycling & Waste | Recycling, waste collection and processing, reuse, repair and circular models, battery and e-waste recycling, waste into non-fuel products. |
| Sustainable Materials | New materials and material-based goods replacing conventional ones: bio-based polymers, packaging, textiles, coatings, advanced materials, sustainable consumer products. |
| Transportation | EVs and components (except batteries), EV charging, micromobility, aviation, marine and rail vehicles, fleet electrification, logistics efficiency, transit and shared mobility. |
| Water | Water and wastewater treatment, desalination, reuse, water quality monitoring, efficiency and metering, stormwater, PFAS in water. |
| Other | Cleantech with a real environmental angle that fits none of the above. |
| Not cleantech | No plausible environmental or resource-efficiency angle at all. |

**Rules, in order:**

1. Classify by what the company makes or does. Use Built Environment, Transportation, AgTech & Food or Industrial only when the product exists solely for that market. (An EV battery maker is Energy Storage; an EV charging network is Transportation.)
2. Software, financing, marketplaces, data and services for a sector belong to that sector.
3. If a company spans several sectors, use the sector of its flagship or first product.
4. Be permissive about cleantech. Any plausible climate, energy, emissions, resource or pollution benefit counts. Use "Not cleantech" only when there is truly no environmental angle.
5. Use "Other" only for real cleantech that fits nowhere else.

**Known boundary cases to watch.** These accounted for most of the Sector disagreement between runs:

- Industrial vs Sustainable Materials
- Energy Storage vs Grid & Power
- Recycling & Waste vs Sustainable Materials

Valuation Cap iThe maximum company valuation used to calculate the note's conversion price. If the next round prices the company above this cap, note holders convert at the cap instead, getting more shares for their investment.
$
Discount iThe percentage discount note holders receive when converting to equity. A 20% discount means investors pay 80% of the price-per-share that new investors pay. Enter the discount amount, not the price paid (i.e., enter 20, not 80).
%
Interest Rate iThe annual interest rate that accrues on the note's principal. This interest typically converts to additional equity (more shares) rather than being paid in cash. Standard rates for convertible notes are 4–8%.
%
Maturity iHow long until the note comes due if it hasn't converted to equity. At maturity, the company must either convert the note, repay it, or negotiate an extension. Typical terms are 18–24 months.

Coverage iThe number of shares the warrant holder can purchase, or the dollar value of warrant coverage as a percentage of the investment. For example, "20% coverage" on a $100K investment gives $20K worth of warrants. Can also be a fixed share count.
Exercise Price iThe price per share the warrant holder pays when exercising (buying) the shares. Usually set at the current share price or at a premium. A lower exercise price is more favorable to the holder.
Expiration iHow long the warrant holder has to exercise their right to buy shares. After this period, unexercised warrants expire worthless. Typical warrant terms range from 5 to 10 years.
